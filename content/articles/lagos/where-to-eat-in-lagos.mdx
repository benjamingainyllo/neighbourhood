Ask ten Lagosians where to eat and you will get eleven answers, two arguments and at least one invitation to someone's aunt's house. Food here is personal, loud and deeply regional, and the best meals are rarely the ones with the longest queues on Instagram.

This guide is built from years of eating across the city: from the mainland bukas that open at dawn to the island dining rooms where young chefs are taking Nigerian ingredients somewhere new. Bring cash, bring patience for traffic, and bring an appetite.

## Start with a buka

A buka is a canteen-style restaurant serving the dishes Lagos actually eats every day: amala and ewedu, pounded yam with egusi, ofada rice with its fierce green-pepper stew. You point at the pots, someone ladles generously, and you eat with your right hand.

**Mama Kemi's Kitchen, Yaba.** A tin-roofed room near the university that fills with students, mechanics and bankers by noon. Order the amala with gbegiri and ewedu, add assorted meat, and do not ask for less pepper.

**The Bukka at Surulere.** Slightly more polished, still unmistakably local. The ofada rice comes wrapped in leaves and the stew is the colour of a sunset.

## After dark: suya and pepper soup

Suya is Lagos's great equaliser: thin strips of beef rubbed in yaji spice, grilled over charcoal and wrapped in newspaper with raw onion and tomato. The best stands set up around sunset and sell out before midnight.

<PullQuote cite="Tolu Adebayo">
  The best suya in Lagos is wherever the queue of taxi drivers is longest.
</PullQuote>

Follow it with a bowl of goat-meat pepper soup at one of the open-air spots along the Lekki corridor. It is fierce, fragrant with uziza and utazi, and widely believed to cure everything from heartbreak to a long flight.

## The new Nigerian kitchen

A generation of chefs, many trained in London or Paris and now back home, are cooking tasting menus built entirely on local produce: smoked catfish, tiger nuts, cocoyam, hibiscus. Expect small plates, natural light and wine lists that take Nigerian palates seriously.

**Ẹ̀kọ́ House, Ikoyi.** Seven courses that move from the creeks of the Niger Delta to the grasslands of the north. Book a week ahead.

**Field Notes, Victoria Island.** Lunch-only, with a menu that changes daily depending on what arrives from the market at 6am.

### Good to know

- Most bukas are cash-only and close by mid-afternoon.
- Tipping around 10% is appreciated in sit-down restaurants.
- Traffic can double travel time; plan meals by neighbourhood, not by list.
