Nairobi's hotel scene used to be split between international chains and safari lodges on the edge of town. That has changed. A wave of small, independently owned hotels now gives travellers somewhere to stay that feels like the city itself: leafy, considered, and quietly full of character.

This guide was produced in partnership with a Kenyan boutique hotel group. As with everything we publish, our editors chose the hotels and wrote every word.

## For garden lovers: The Fig House, Karen

A converted 1950s family home set in two acres of indigenous garden, twenty minutes from the national park. Eight rooms, a long wooden veranda and breakfast that arrives with fresh passion-fruit juice and the sound of hornbills.

## For remote workers: Kilimani Lofts

Serviced apartments with proper desks, fast fibre and backup power: the holy trinity for anyone working from Nairobi. The rooftop has views to the Ngong Hills and a coffee bar that opens at seven.

## For design lovers: Hotel Westlands

Twenty-four rooms filled with furniture and art commissioned from Kenyan makers, a few minutes' walk from the galleries and restaurants of Westlands. The ground-floor restaurant is worth a visit even if you are staying elsewhere.

### Before you book

- Karen and Langata are greener and quieter but further from the centre.
- Westlands and Kilimani are best for restaurants and nightlife.
- Ask about backup power and water if you are working remotely.
