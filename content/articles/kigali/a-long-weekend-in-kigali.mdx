Kigali is a compact, easy city to fall for, and one of the few African capitals where three days feels genuinely relaxed rather than rushed. Flights from London, Amsterdam and Brussels connect in a single stop, the city is safe to walk, and the pace is gentle enough that you'll come home rested.

Here's how we'd spend a long weekend, with room left over for wandering.

## Day one: coffee and context

Begin with a slow breakfast in Kiyovu, then spend the late morning at the Kigali Genocide Memorial. It is a difficult visit and an essential one: almost nothing about the city makes sense without it. Afterwards, walk down through the leafy streets towards the centre and stop for lunch somewhere with a view.

In the afternoon, visit one of the city's specialty coffee roasters for a tasting. Rwandan coffee is bright, fruity and grown on small family plots; many roasters will tell you exactly which hill each bag came from.

## Day two: makers and markets

Head to Nyamirambo, the city's oldest and liveliest neighbourhood, for a walking tour led by local women. Then browse Kimironko Market for printed fabrics; a tailor can turn them into a shirt or dress within a day or two.

## Day three: out to the lake

If you have a car and driver, the road to Lake Muhazi takes about an hour and winds through some of the prettiest countryside in the region. Spend the afternoon by the water and be back in Kigali for sundowners on a rooftop.

## Practical notes

- Most visitors can get a visa on arrival; check the latest rules before you travel.
- Card payments are common in the city, but carry some cash for markets and moto-taxis.
- Plastic bags are banned; pack a tote.
