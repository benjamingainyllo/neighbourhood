Adaeze Okafor's studio is on the second floor of a former printing works in Yaba, up a staircase that smells of dye and diesel. Bolts of hand-woven aso-oke lean against every wall. Two looms clatter in the corner. On a good day, she says, the generator only cuts out twice.

Three years ago she was a senior designer at a fashion house in London. Today she runs a twelve-person studio that works with weavers in Iseyin, the historic centre of aso-oke production, and sells to boutiques in Paris, Tokyo and New York. We talked about leaving, landing and learning to make things in Lagos.

## What made you come back?

"I was designing prints inspired by West African textiles for a European brand, and one day it hit me: I was copying something I could go and learn properly. My grandmother wove. Her loom was still in the family house. It felt ridiculous to be doing this from a desk in Shoreditch."

<PullQuote cite="Adaeze Okafor">
  In London I was designing about Lagos. Here, I get to design with it.

## What surprised you most?

"How fast everything moves. A tailor in Yaba will turn around a sample in two days that would take three weeks in Europe. The energy is incredible. The infrastructure is not. You learn to plan for the power cut, the traffic and the delivery that arrives a week late, and then you get on with it."

## Where do you go for inspiration in the city?

"Balogun Market, early, before the crowds. The colour combinations people wear there are braver than anything on a runway. Then I'll go to one of the galleries on the island, or just sit with the weavers and listen."

## Advice for creatives visiting Lagos?

"Come with time, not a checklist. Say yes to the wedding invitation. Visit the studios: most of us are happy to show people around if you message first. And buy directly from makers when you can. It matters more than you think."
